[Atelectasis | Definition]
Atelectasis is partial or complete collapse of part of the lung, so that area contains less air than normal.

[Atelectasis | X-ray appearance]
It typically appears as a loss of lung volume, seen as linear or plate-like white streaks, a shifted lung fissure, or nearby structures being pulled toward the collapsed area, such as the diaphragm rising or the central chest structures shifting.

[Atelectasis | Common causes]
Common underlying causes include a blocked airway from mucus or a tumor, shallow breathing after surgery or with pain, and external compression of the lung, for example by fluid or air in the pleural space.

[Atelectasis | Often seen with]
Frequently observed alongside pleural effusion, since fluid pressing on the lung is a common cause of collapse in the area next to it.

[Atelectasis | Look-alikes]
Can overlap closely with pneumonia and consolidation in appearance, since all three can produce whitening of the lung; the pattern of volume loss is what helps tell atelectasis apart.

[Atelectasis | Limits of the X-ray]
Small or subtle areas of collapse can be difficult to see on a standard frontal film, and an X-ray cannot always identify the underlying cause, such as whether an airway is blocked by mucus or a mass.
