[Cardiomegaly | Definition]
Cardiomegaly means the heart appears enlarged on imaging, beyond what is expected for a person's chest size.

[Cardiomegaly | X-ray appearance]
On a standard upright, front-facing (PA) film, it typically appears as a heart width greater than about half the total width of the chest, a measurement called the cardiothoracic ratio. The heart's overall silhouette looks rounded or enlarged compared with a normal, more tapered outline.

[Cardiomegaly | Common causes]
Common underlying causes include high blood pressure, weakened heart muscle (cardiomyopathy), valve disease, and fluid collecting around the heart (pericardial effusion).

[Cardiomegaly | Often seen with]
Frequently observed alongside pulmonary edema and pleural effusion, since an enlarged, poorly functioning heart can cause fluid to back up into the lungs and pleural space.

[Cardiomegaly | Look-alikes]
Can be confused with a normal heart that only looks large because of positioning, or with fluid around the heart rather than true enlargement of the heart muscle itself.

[Cardiomegaly | Limits of the X-ray]
Portable, AP-view, or supine films, and films taken during a shallow breath, all tend to make the heart look larger than it truly is, so the cardiothoracic ratio is only reliable on a proper upright PA film. An X-ray also shows size, not how well the heart is pumping.
